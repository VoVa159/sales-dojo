# Methodology (original summary of Sandler principles)

## Core mindset
- Equal business stature: two professionals checking fit.
  No begging, no over-thanking.
- "No" is a good outcome. "I'll think about it" is not.
- Buyers decide emotionally (Child), justify logically (Adult),
  and check it's allowed (Parent). The seller stays in Adult.
- Identity vs role: a bad call is a bad performance, not a bad
  person. This is what lets you ask hard questions.
- No free consulting: no demo, quote or solution before pain,
  budget and decision are clear.
- Talk 30%, listen 70%.

## The Submarine (7 sealed compartments, in order)
1. Bonding & rapport
2. Up-Front Contract (UFC)
3. Pain
4. Budget
5. Decision
6. Fulfillment (present only what solves confirmed pain)
7. Post-sell (lock it in, prevent buyer's remorse)
Don't move on until the current one is sealed.

## Up-Front Contract – 5 parts
Time ("We have about 30 minutes – still work?"), purpose,
their agenda, your agenda (permission to ask hard questions),
outcome (yes with a dated next step, or a clean no). End with
"Fair?" and wait for the yes. Use a UFC at the start of every
call, before every proposal, and at the end for the next step.

## Pain – three levels
1. Surface: the technical problem. "What's happening today?"
   Go deeper: "Give me an example from last week." "How often?"
2. Business: a number. Let the buyer do the maths:
   "So 1,500 a week – what's that over a year?"
   No number = level 2 not found.
3. Personal: "Where were you when it happened?"
   "What happens if it's the same in two years?" Then silence.
Capacity questions (ask, don't tell): "If you had those hours
back, what would you do with them?"
Summarise the pain in their words, then ask the commitment
question: "Is this something you want to fix now, or have you
learned to live with it?"

## Questioning tools
- Reversing: answer a question with a softened question.
  "Good question – what made you ask that now?"
- Dummy curve: "Help me understand..." Curious, not clever.
- Negative reverse: pull toward no so they push toward yes.
  "Honestly, I don't know yet if we can help." Then silence.
- No seagulls: never pitch features they didn't ask for.

## Budget
- Bracket early: "Projects like this run between X and Y.
  Where does that sit for you?" Then stay silent.
- "What's a fortune to you?" / "How did you get to that number?"
- Separate price from cash flow. Lock a conditional yes before
  offering terms: "If we found a structure that doesn't hurt
  your cash flow, would you move forward?" ("move forward",
  not "consider").

## Decision
- "Apart from you, who else needs to be involved for us to
  move forward?" Then name the user/technical person.
- How did they decide last time? By when? What if it slips?
- Watch for the hidden veto (the daily user who prefers the
  competitor).
- Turn the internal contact into a champion: "When your boss
  asks why spend this, what will you tell him?"

## Fulfillment
Present max 3 capabilities, each tied to a pain they said.
Thermometer: "1 to 10, where are you now? What makes it a 10?"

## Post-sell
"What could happen between now and our next meeting that makes
you think twice?" "What will you say if the competitor calls
with 20% off?"

## Classic objections
- "I need to think about it" → "Usually that's a polite no.
  Is that what this is?"
- "Too expensive" → "Fair. What were you expecting, and what
  are you comparing it to?"
- "We're happy with our supplier" → "Sounds like I should stop
  calling, right?"
- "Send me something" → "Happy to. What specifically do you
  want to see, and when will you decide?"
- Ghosting → break-up message: "I assume this is off your list.
  Should I close the file?" Then don't chase.
